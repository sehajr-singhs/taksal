About

About Taksal

A research home for Sikh knowledge, open to everyone.

Taksal is a place to read and cite Sikh sources: Gurbani, the Dasam Granth, the Vaaran of Bhai Gurdas Ji, historical texts, hukamnamas, digital pothis, translations and scholarship. The aim is simple. A reader should be able to find a line, read it in the form they need, and cite it exactly.

The book that started it

The idea came from reading Sicques, Tigers, or Thieves: Eyewitness Accounts of the Sikhs (1606-1810), edited by Amandeep Singh Madra and Parmjit Singh. The editors spent years finding, translating and annotating accounts that were scattered across archives and rare books. Taksal is meant to make that kind of research faster for everyone, and to keep the sources visible while it does.

The plan

An open corpus. Sikh texts, translations and historical works in one place, with clear provenance and stable citations.

OCR for old manuscripts. A pipeline using Surya OCR and open vision language models to turn scans of pothis and old printed editions into searchable text, checked by human readers.

A cited model. Taksal (the model), which answers only with citations to the sources on the site, refuses when no source supports an answer, shows multiple traditions' interpretations side by side, and never invents Gurbani.

Taksal is independent and not affiliated with Damdami Taksal or any jatha.

Corrections

Source texts come from BaniDB. If a line looks wrong, write to the team with the ang, line number and the edition you are checking against. Corrections to the catalog and to the site are welcome in the same way.
